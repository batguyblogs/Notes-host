## Logistic Regression
It is a supervised learning algorithm and is used for **Classification problems**. Whereas *Dependant variable* is categorical and binary. 

$$
P = \dfrac{1}{1+e^{-z}}
$$
where
$$
z = wx+b
$$
### Gradient Decent Algorithm for *Regression*
Let $\hat{y}$ be the predicted output from the model, and $y$ be the actual output.
Error $e$ is given by
$$
e = \hat{y}-y
$$
The Objective function $J$ is given by
$$
J =\frac{1}{2}e^{2}
$$
The gradient Descents are
$$
\frac{\delta J}{\delta w} , \frac{\delta J}{\delta b}
$$
and the weights
$$
w_{\text{new}}=w_{\text{old}}-\eta\frac{\delta J}{\delta w}
$$
$$
b_{\text{new}}=b_{old}-\eta\frac{ \delta J}{\delta b}
$$
where $\eta$ is the learning rate
$$
\eta = 0.1\  (\text{usually})
$$
now
$$
\frac{\delta J}{\delta w}=\frac{\delta J}{\delta e}\times \frac{\delta e}{\delta w} = e \times x 
$$
$$
\boxed{\implies \frac{\delta J}{\delta w} = ex}
$$
similarly 
$$
\frac{\delta J}{\delta b}  = \frac{\delta J}{\delta e} \times \frac{\delta e}{\delta b} = e \times 1
$$
$$
\boxed{\implies \frac{\delta J}{\delta b} = e}
$$
### Gradient Decent for **Classification**
The objective function $J$ is given by
$$
J = - \left[y \ln P + (1-y)\ln(1-P)\right]
$$
$$
\frac{\delta J}{\delta w} \ \& \ \frac{\delta J}{\delta b}
$$
$$
\frac{\delta J}{\delta w} = \frac{\delta J}{\delta P} \times \frac{\delta P}{\delta z} \times \frac{\delta z}{\delta w}
$$
$$
\frac{\delta J}{\delta b} = \frac{\delta J}{\delta P}. \frac{\delta P}{\delta z}. \frac{\delta z}{\delta b}
$$
now
$$
\frac{\delta J}{\delta P} = - \left[ \frac{y}{P} - \frac{1-y}{1-P} \right] = \frac{P-y}{P(1-P)}
$$
$$
\frac{\delta P}{\delta z} = \frac{e^{-z}}{(1+e^{-z})} 
$$
